## 1. Frame — 1st-gen aluminum Stigmata

- TIG-welded aluminum, nominally round-profile **Easton EA6X** tubing. Santa Cruz's
  entry into the US cyclocross boom; middle-of-the-road CX geometry, compact.
- **Cantilever bosses rear** — never a disc frame. The front disc on this
  build exists only because of an aftermarket fork (§3).
- **Bottom bracket standard: ** — English threaded BSA 68 m
- Rear spacing: 130 mm QR road

### Tire clearance — measured on this bike

| End | Max fitted | Headroom | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rear** (alu frame) | **40 mm tubeless** | **~2 mm** each side | No mud room. Aluminium — **cannot be cold-set / stretched**; 40 mm is the hard ceiling. Factory rating was ~35 mm knobby. |
| **Front** (replaced fork) | ~**43 mm** | a few mm | Fork swap bought back some clearance vs. the rear. 40mm fits with mud clearance |

### 1x setup notes
- Chain line: a road 1x wants the ring roughly centred; a 38–42T **narrow-wide** is the
  usual pick for a gravel/CX 1x with a 12–32.
- Range on 40T × 12–32 ≈ 34–108 gear-inches. For CX racing a 38–40T is typical; for
  gravel races with real climbing, 38T or a wider cassette (11–36 with an MTB mech).
- 10sp road/MTB mechs are mostly **non-clutch** — chain security on rough race courses
  depends on the narrow-wide ring plus a **chain guide / top guide** on the old FD tab.
  A clutched 10sp MTB mech (e.g. later X9/X0 Type 2) is the bigger upgrade for racing.

## 3. Brakes — mismatched front/rear ("mullet brakes")

### Front — Avid BB7 mechanical disc on an aftermarket disc fork
- **Fork:** replaced the original canti carbon fork to add a front disc. Per earlier
  notes this is a **Winwood Muddy Carbon Cross** (disc tabs + canti bosses, 1‑1/8" alloy
  steerer) — but the measured **~43 mm tire clearance** is wider than a typical CX fork,
  so **confirm whether it's still the Winwood or a later gravel-fork replacement**, and
  record axle-to-crown and offset. Winwood is defunct; a modern alloy/carbon disc gravel
  fork with matching A-C (~395–400 mm) and offset (~45 mm) is the replacement path.
- **Caliper:** Avid BB7. With drop-bar Force levers this **must be the BB7 *Road***
  version (shorter cable pull). If a BB7 *Mountain* is fitted, lever throw is wrong
  (grabby / bottoms out) — **verify** and swap to Road if needed. Rotor likely 160 mm.
- Service: set the inner (fixed) pad to a light rub first, then the outer red dial;
  re-dial every few mm of wear. Compressionless housing to the caliper helps markedly.
  Pads: organic (quiet, wet-friendly) or semi-metallic (longer life in grit).

### Rear — cantilever with Kool Stop pads
- Frame canti bosses; drop-bar straddle setup.
- **Kool Stop** pads — Salmon compound for wet rims. Toe-in ~1 mm; balance L/R
  spring tension; straddle height sets power vs clearance (lower yoke = more power).
- The rear rim is a **wear item** braking on wet grit — check the rim wear indicator /
  measure sidewall thickness periodically (the custom rear wheel is worth keeping, §4).

## 4. Wheels & tires — mixed set

| | Front | Rear |
|---|---|---|
| Build | **Easton EA70 XC** (29er, disc) factory wheel | **custom hand-build** by a local wheel builder |
| Hub | Easton C/V-series | **White Industries** (10sp Shimano/SRAM freehub) — model TBD |
| Rim | Easton EA70 | **Alex** (model TBD) |
| Brake | disc, 6-bolt | rim (cantilever surface) |

- **Front hub (Easton EA70 XC):** this generation is known for freehub/bearing wear —
  service at the first sign of play or a notchy freehub; kits still available.
- **Rear hub (White Industries):** yearly teardown — clean & regrease pawls and drive
  ring with **Lubriplate No. 105** (light grease; heavy grease makes pawls skip).
  Freehub bodies ship without pawls; **never mix pawls with the wrong ratchet ring**
  (hub failure). 24 vs 48 pt pawl sets ~$20. Very rebuildable — expect it to outlast
  rims.
- **Record when the shop has the rear apart:** rim model, spoke count, spoke lengths
  (DS/NDS), lacing pattern, build date.
- **Tubeless:** **DIY conversion on both wheels** (owner-installed rim tape + valves
  from a conversion kit) on rims that were **not** originally tubeless-ready — the front
  Easton EA70 XC (plain, non-tubeless model; tape reads "Tubeless Ready" but that's the
  tape product's own branding, not an OEM rim sticker — likely a conversion Doug did and
  forgot about) and the rear custom Alex-rim build. Sealing very well most of the time —
  holds air better than the factory-tubeless Bontrager Paradigm wheels on the
  Checkpoint (see the front-wheel roll-off incident in §5 for the failure mode when it
  doesn't). Note each rim's **internal width** — it drives mounted tire width, which
  matters with only ~2 mm rear clearance and with a front rim (29er MTB width) sized
  wider than the CX tires now mounted on it.
  - Converted (non-TLR) rims: the tape *is* the airtight layer, so inspect it at every
    tire change; re-tape if it lifts at the valve or nipple holes. A dry, well-taped
    conversion can burp at very low pressure — keep race pressures sane for the tire width.
- **Race tires:**
  - Dry: **40 mm tubeless** rear (hard ceiling), up to ~43 mm front. File-tread /
    low-profile for gravel; a proper CX tread for cross. Conti Terra speed
  - Wet/mud: need to spec something

## 5. Wet / muddy race setup

**Tire: WTB Cross Boss 700x35c, TCS tubeless, front and rear** (purchased 2026-09-13).
Picked for simplicity (one tire both ends) and mud-shedding tread at a width that
respects the frame's clearance (40mm hard rear ceiling, less once mud packs in — see
§0). Not a UCI-width concern; Doug races non-UCI-sanctioned events. If it doesn't hook
up well enough in deep slop, the fallback would be a dedicated mud-spike tread
(Vittoria Terreno T90 Mud, Donnelly PDX, IRC Serac CX Mud) — those mostly top out at
33mm since they're built around the UCI-race-width market, and narrower also cuts
through mud better than wider.

Run with the Vittoria Air-Liner Gravel inserts (ordered 2026-09-13, one per wheel, ×2)
front and rear — see §4 tubeless notes and the front-wheel roll-off incident above for
why.

**Incident (2026-09-13):** Ran 700x40 front / 700x35 rear tubeless, ~50 psi on two
commute rides with no issues. At a race, let air out **by feel, without a gauge**, did
warm-up laps, and on a corner-plus-bump the **front** tire rolled off the bead and
flatted instantly — DNS.

**Correction: this was the FRONT wheel (Easton EA70 XC).** Confirmed the rim is the
plain **EA70 XC**, not the XCT — Easton's standard, non-tubeless model in this lineup.
There's tape on the rim printed "Tubeless Ready," but that's almost certainly the rim
tape *product's* branding (Stan's, WTB TCS, Orange Seal, etc. all print that on the
tape itself), not an OEM sticker declaring the rim factory tubeless. **Most likely
conclusion: this front wheel is also a DIY tape conversion on a non-TLR rim** — same
category as the rear wheel, just apparently done by Doug at some point and forgotten.
That reactivates the original diagnosis:
- **Non-TLR rim, DIY tape conversion:** bead retention depends more heavily on air
  pressure alone than it would on a true tubeless-ready rim with proper bead-lock/hook
  geometry.
- **Rim width vs. tire width:** this is a 29er MTB rim (~19–20mm internal), originally
  spec'd for ~1.9–2.3" MTB tires, now running a 40mm (~1.57") cyclocross tire — a
  narrower tire than the rim was designed around. That can change the tire's profile
  and bead angle in a way that makes it easier to unseat under a hard cornering+bump
  load, even on a genuinely tubeless-ready rim.
- **Unmeasured low pressure** is still very likely a contributing factor regardless of
  rim type: letting air out by feel means the actual pressure at the moment of failure
  is unknown, and a 40mm tire at low CX pressure has a real minimum below which a
  cornering+bump hit will unseat the bead.
- **Bead seating:** only 2 rides on this tire before the race — worth checking that the
  bead was fully and evenly seated (visible seating line all the way around), since an
  incompletely seated bead can survive gentle commuting but fail under race loads.

**Action items before racing this wheel tubeless again:**
- [ ] Inspect the front rim tape at the next tire change — check brand/condition, and
  re-tape if it's lifting anywhere, same as the rear conversion.
- [ ] Always set race pressure with a gauge and record it here (never by feel).
- [ ] Establish a tested safe minimum pressure for a 40mm tire on this front rim and
  treat it as a floor, not a target.
- [ ] Re-check/re-seat the front tire bead evenly all the way around before the next race.
- [ ] Consider a tire insert (e.g. CushCore XC/Gravel or similar) up front for races —
  adds bead security at low pressure, at the cost of some weight.

## 6. Open items to record

- [ ] 1x chainring: single ring size, narrow-wide or not, chain guide fitted?
- [ ] FSA crank model
- [ ] White Industries rear hub model + last service date
- [ ] Front rim internal width (for mounted tire width)
- [x] Front fork axle type: **confirmed QR 100mm** (Winwood fork).
- [ ] Backup race wheelset: since this bike is disc-front / cantilever-rim-brake-rear,
  an off-the-shelf "wheelset" won't fit both ends — buying two separate wheels instead:
  - **Front:** any 100mm QR, 6-bolt disc wheel. Common and cheap — QR disc predates the
    thru-axle era, so there's a large used/new pool (generic disc-QR gravel/MTB
    wheels).
  - **Rear:** 130mm QR, rim-brake-compatible (cantilever doesn't care about rim
    profile — any rim with a brake track works), Shimano/SRAM HG freehub. **Does not
    need to be 10-speed-specific** — any 8/9/10/11-speed HG freehub works with the
    10sp cassette; on an 11-speed body add a ~$10 1.85mm + 1.0mm spacer kit behind the
    cassette. This opens the search to basically any pre-2019 rim-brake road wheel
    (Shimano RS010/RS100/R501, Mavic Aksium, Fulcrum Racing 7/8), new (Shimano
    WH-RS100 still sold as of 2026) or used/take-off.
  - Spare rear especially should probably run a tube, not tubeless, as race-day
    insurance (see §5 incident).
  - If new/used sourcing dries up, fallback is building the rear from parts (QR
    rim-brake hub + Mavic Open Pro or Velocity A23 rim + spokes) — guarantees fit
    since Doug builds/services his own wheels.
