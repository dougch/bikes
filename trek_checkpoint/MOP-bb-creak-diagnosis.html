Bottom-Bracket-Area Creak — Diagnosis & Correction

Bike
Trek Checkpoint SL 6 AXS Gen 2 (2023), 54 cm
Assembly
SRAM Rival 1 AXS crank · DUB Wide spindle · SRAM DUB T47 bottom bracket (cup reads "SRAM"/"DUB" — confirm Road vs Road Wide)
Symptom
Sharp creak at crank 3 / 9 o'clock, under load only. Reproducible statically (clipped in, braced, brakes locked). Onset immediately after a shop BB replacement.
Ruled out
Pedals and cleats — confirmed by left↔right swap, no change.

01 Safety & ground rules

  • Bike in a stand. The chain comes off — have a spare SRAM Flattop quick link.
  • Do not exceed any torque value. Under-torque creaks; over-torque cracks carbon or strips alloy. When unsure, use the lower number and re-check.
  • Keep DOT fluid, degreaser, and solvents off the brake rotors and pads.
Stop — read before you start

This creak followed a paid service. Opening the BB yourself may end any recourse with the original installer. If that matters, shoot a phone video of the static creak now, before any disassembly.

Exploded view of the crank / bottom-bracket assembly, non-drive (left) side at left. Generic SRAM DUB + T47 — your parts match this layout. Not to scale.

  • 8
    Drive crank + spider + 40T ringThe 8 chainring bolts thread in from behind the spider — crank must come off to reach them.

03 Tools & materials

Tools

  • 8 mm hex bit + torque wrench (to ~55 N·m)
  • T25 Torx bit
  • 2 mm and 2.5 mm hex keys — check which the preload clamp screw takes
  • Low-range torque wrench, 1–10 N·m, for chainring bolts
  • T47 BB wrench — the installed BB is a SRAM DUB T47 (cup reads "SRAM"/"DUB"):
    • Use Park Tool BBT-47-16 — the 16-notch 52–54 mm T47 tool that lists "SRAM T47 DUB". 3/8" drive (takes a torque wrench). Equivalents: SRAM's own DUB T47 tool, or 16-notch T47 tools from Wheels Mfg / Unior / Birzman.
    • Not the 44 mm external tool (Park BBT-69.4 / BBT-79) — same 16-notch pattern, wrong diameter; that fits DUB BSA, not T47.
    • Not the 12-notch Park BBT-47 (no "-16") — that's for Chris King ThreadFit.
  • Soft mallet · bright light · dental / bearing pick · calipers (optional)

Materials

  • Bike grease (spindle splines, non-structural threads)
  • Anti-seize or assembly grease for the T47 threads
  • Blue threadlocker (chainring bolts, rotor bolts)
  • Friction / carbon assembly paste (optional — ring-to-spider face)
  • Isopropyl alcohol · clean rags · nitrile gloves

05 Procedure

STEP 0

Chain-slack isolation test — before any disassembly

  1. Shift to a mid gear. Push the derailleur cage forward to fully slacken the chain, or lift the chain off the ring onto the BB shell.
  2. Brace, clip in, lock the brakes, rock the cranks hard at 3 / 9 o'clock.
Branch on the result

Creak gone with slack chain → chain-tension path: chainring bolts, ring/spider interface, chain, cassette lockring, freehub, rear hub. Focus Steps 2 and 7.

Creak still present → spindle splines, BB cups/bearings, thread prep, or frame. Focus Steps 3–4.

Optional confirm: drip light oil at one junction at a time (cup-to-frame seam, behind the chainring, pedal threads, seat collar). If the creak stops for a few minutes after wetting one joint, that joint is the source.

STEP 1

Remove the crank

  1. Loosen the preload collar clamp screw (part 4). Back the collar (part 3) off ~2 turns by hand.
  2. If the non-drive arm has two T25 pinch bolts (most road DUB cranks do not), loosen both now.
  3. 8 mm hex into the crank bolt. Unscrew — the self-extracting cap draws the arm off. Remove it.
  4. Slide the drive-side crank + spindle out through the shell. Soft-mallet the spindle end if snug. Note the feel — it should slide, not fight.
STEP 2

Chainring & spider

  1. Before touching a bolt: inspect the ring-to-spider mating faces. Grey/black oxide paste or bright fretting scars = micro-movement = a prime creak source.
  2. Remove the 8 T25 bolts one at a time. Clean each bolt + hole with isopropyl, one drop blue threadlocker, reinstall finger-tight.
  3. If fretting was present: separate the ring, clean both faces, thin film of anti-seize or friction paste on the contact land only, reassemble.
  4. Torque the 8 bolts in a star pattern, two passes, to spec (~2.5 N·m — confirm).
  5. Grab the ring at 12 and 6 and try to rock it on the spider. Any movement = not fixed; revisit face prep.
  6. Inspect the spindle-to-spider junction for cracks or movement (rare — warranty item if found).
STEP 3

Spindle & bearings

  1. Wipe the spindle splines clean. Look for galling, wear, or a dry scuffed look.
  2. Spin each BB bearing with a fingertip: must be smooth and silent. Notchy / gritty / rough = contaminated during install (common if the bike was pressure-washed afterward).
  3. Push–pull and rock each bearing radially. Any play = bearing or seat is bad.
  4. Check bearing seals for purged grease or water/grit ingress.

Rough bearings or play → BB comes out (Step 4). Bearings perfect → still do Step 4's torque + thread check.

STEP 4

T47 bottom bracket cups

  1. Put the BBT-47-16 on each cup, gently test in the loosening direction. Movement before ~35 N·m = it was under-torqued. Thread direction: follow the directional arrows stamped on the SRAM cups (drive side is typically left-hand / reverse).
  2. Remove both cups. Inspect: threads must carry grease/anti-seize — bone-dry = the classic T47 creak. No galling, cross-threading, or alloy shavings; cups should thread back most of the way by hand.
  3. Confirm the variant. The cup reads "SRAM"/"DUB" — confirm it's the Road Wide version (matches your DUB Wide crank), not the standard Road. A standard-width DUB BB under a Wide crank never fully stops creaking; if it's wrong, fit the Road Wide.
  4. Clean, fresh anti-seize, reinstall, torque 35–50 N·m per the cup arrows.
Prime suspect

Given the history — creak on both arms, static-reproducible, immediately after the service — a dry or under-torqued cup and a wrong (non-Wide) BB are the two most likely findings here.

STEP 5

Reassemble

  1. Thin film of grease on the spindle splines and the non-drive arm bore.
  2. Slide the drive-side crank + spindle through the shell. Fit the non-drive arm.
  3. 8 mm hex, torque the crank bolt to 54 N·m.
  4. Set preload: thread the collar in by hand until it just contacts — no lateral crank rock, cranks still spin freely. Then nip the clamp screw just enough to hold it (~1.5 N·m). If your crank uses non-drive pinch bolts instead, torque those to ~8–9 N·m.
  5. Reinstall the chain (new Flattop quick link if broken).
STEP 6

Retest

  1. Brace, clip in, lock brakes, load the cranks hard at 3 / 9 both directions.
  2. Ride under load out of the saddle.
Acceptance

No creak under static load and no creak on a loaded climb. If clear → done; log it (Step 8). If not → Step 7.

STEP 7

If the creak survives Steps 1–6

Work these in order; retest after each:

  1. Seatpost / Reverb dropper — pull it fully, clean seat tube + post, thin grease film, reinstall, binder torque low (just past slip). Clears many phantom "BB" creaks.
  2. Saddle rail clamp — check the dropper head bolts to spec, grease the rail cradle.
  3. Thru-axles — remove front & rear, grease threads + under head, torque 12–15 N·m.
  4. Bottle cage bolts — remove, grease, 3 N·m.
  5. IsoSpeed pivot — check the decoupler bolt against the Trek manual; a dry IsoSpeed bearing creaks under pedaling load and gets blamed on the BB constantly.
  6. Chain & quick link — check wear with a Flattop-specific gauge; a worn or dry link ticks under load.
  7. Cassette lockring / freehub — pull the cassette, grease the lockring threads and freehub splines, torque the lockring to spec.
STEP 8

Close-out log

Record in specs.md or a maintenance log: date · symptom · what was found · what fixed it · installed BB (SRAM DUB T47 — Road vs Road Wide?) · final torque values · parts replaced.

06 Most likely root causes

Ranked for this bike and this history (both arms, static-reproducible, immediately post-service):

  1. Preload collar backed off or set wrong during the service
  2. T47 cups installed dry / under-torqued
  3. Crank spindle splines reinstalled dry
  4. DUB Wide vs standard DUB BB mismatch
  5. Chainring bolts disturbed / not threadlocked (if Step 0 pointed at the chain-tension path)
  6. Contaminated BB bearing (if the bike was washed after the service)
